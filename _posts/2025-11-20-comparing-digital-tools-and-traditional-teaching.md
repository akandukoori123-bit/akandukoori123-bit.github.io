For this project I ran a comparative study of **digital math tools versus
traditional instruction**, measuring which approach produced stronger learning
gains.

Instead of arguing about it, I tried to measure it: structured comparisons of
learning outcomes between technology-based methods and conventional teaching. The
data pointed clearly toward **significantly higher learning gains with the
technology-based methods** — which has real implications for how schools spend
limited budgets.

The work became a research publication on **arXiv**. Turning a classroom question
into a documented, reproducible study taught me how to design a fair comparison,
analyze the results honestly, and write them up so other people can check the
reasoning.
