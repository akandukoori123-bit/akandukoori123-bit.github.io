One of my favorite hands-on biology projects was **extracting DNA from
strawberries** — isolating real genetic material with equipment you can actually
get your hands on.

The process walks through the fundamentals of wet-lab biology: pulverizing the
tissue to break cells open, using **micropipetting** for precise volumes, and
**centrifugation** to separate the genetic material out. Done right, you end up
with strands of DNA you can see.

I documented the whole thing — procedure, observations, and conclusions — in a lab
journal. The point wasn't just the cool result; it was practicing the habits of
careful experimental work: measure precisely, write down what actually happened,
and let the notes speak for themselves.
