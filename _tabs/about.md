I'm Aditya Kandukoori, a student in the Science, Math &amp; Computer Science magnet
program at Poolesville High School. I like building things and figuring out how
they work — whether that's writing code, wiring up sensors, running a science
experiment, or teaching a classroom of younger students.

I'm the President of **MoCo Innovation**, a STEM-education nonprofit that brings
technology education to students who don't always get access to it. I've helped
raise **$32,000+** in sponsorships to expand our programming across multiple
schools, and I teach coding and computational thinking to elementary and K–12
students through both in-person and online programs.

A lot of my work lives at the intersection of computer science and research. I
built **Biasmap** to study societal bias in large language models by comparing
their outputs against real-world sentiment from news and online discourse, and I
co-authored a study comparing digital math tools with traditional instruction
that was published on **arXiv**. I also like hands-on engineering: a 3D-printed,
EMG-controlled prosthetic arm (**Falconwing**), a triangulation device for
indirect measurement, and a long list of biology and chemistry experiments
written up in lab journals.

Outside of all that, you'll usually find me training in **taekwondo**, in the
pool **swimming**, at the **table-tennis** table, or making **art**. I've also
logged 200+ volunteer hours through food drives and summer-school volunteering.
